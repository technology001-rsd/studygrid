✅ Answer: a) 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg²

The gravitational constant G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg². It is the same throughout the universe and was measured by Cavendish through experiments.

✅ Answer: b) 9.8 m/s²

On Earth, g = 9.8 m/s². On the Moon, it is 1.6 m/s², which is about 1/6 of Earth's gravity.

  • a) 1/2
  • b) 1/4
  • c) 1/6
  • d) 1/10
✅ Answer: c) 1/6

Due to the Moon's lower mass and radius, its g is 1/6 of Earth's.

  • a) vₑ = v₀
  • b) vₑ = √2 v₀
  • c) vₑ = 2 v₀
  • d) vₑ = v₀/√2
✅ Answer: b) vₑ = √2 v₀

Escape velocity = √(2GM/R) and orbital velocity = √(GM/R); therefore vₑ = √2 × v₀.

  • a) T ∝ r
  • b) T² ∝ r³
  • c) T³ ∝ r²
  • d) T ∝ r²
✅ Answer: b) T² ∝ r³

According to Kepler's third law, T² ∝ r³ (the square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the orbital radius).

Identify the Wrong Pair
✅ Wrong Pair: (4) Galileo – Gravitational constant G

The value of gravitational constant G was measured by Cavendish through experiments; Galileo did early work in gravitation but did not measure G.

✅ Wrong Pair: (4) Centrifugal force – Gravitational force

Gravitational force provides the centripetal force for satellites; centrifugal force is a pseudo force arising from inertia, not gravitational force.

Detailed Answers
✅ Answer:
According to Newton's universal law of gravitation, every object in the universe attracts every other object. This attractive force is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: F = G m₁m₂/r². Here G is the universal gravitational constant (6.67×10⁻¹¹ Nm²/kg²). This theory explains the motion of planets, satellites, the Moon, and the Sun.
✅ Answer:
An artificial satellite is a man-made machine that revolves around Earth in a fixed orbit. Applications: (1) Telecommunications – radio, TV, phone messages; (2) Weather forecasting and storm information; (3) Earth observation and mapping; (4) Defense and navigation (GPS).
✅ Answer:
Orbital velocity (v₀ = √(GM/R)) is the minimum velocity required for a satellite to revolve around Earth in a circular orbit. Escape velocity (vₑ = √(2GM/R)) is the velocity required for an object to completely free itself from Earth's gravity and go into space. vₑ = √2 × v₀.
