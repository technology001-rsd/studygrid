Write a Note
The tradition of Marathi historiography. The writer of a king's and clan's chronicle is called a 'Bakharkar'. Many bakhar narrate the life of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and the Maratha kingdom.
The method of historiography during the Mughal period. Records of court events, wars, and administration were kept in 'Tarikh'. Abul Fazl's 'Akbarnama' is an excellent example of this method.

Complete the Concept Map

Long Answers
Kalhana, in 'Rajatarangini', narrated the history of Kashmir's kings; he maintained neutrality and truthfulness. Banabhatta, in 'Harshacharita', described the life and work of Emperor Harsha in detail. Both produced excellent historiography in Sanskrit.
(1) Importance to royal biographies and genealogies. (2) Inclusion of religious and mythological references. (3) Poetic and beautiful literary style. (4) Inclusion of folk tradition (powada, bakhar) and inscriptions (stone inscriptions, copper plates).
Bakhar is the Marathi tradition of writing chronicles of kings (e.g., Shivarajbhushan, Paramaladas). Tarikh is the Mughal court historiographical method; court events were recorded in Persian (e.g., Akbarnama).
Powadas are a medium that records the valour of heroes and historical events through folk songs. Powadas of Tanaji, Prataprao Gujar, Baji Prabhu are oral sources of history.
